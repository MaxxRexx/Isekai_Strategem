Right away, it feels like Border

You had me at the vocabulary. Trion as the resource that powers everything, Triggers as your equippable tools, Black Triggers as the rare, ultimate gear, three-operator squads, and a rank ladder. Even better: the trigger classes line up with the manga's roles almost one-to-one -

attackerAttacker (Kogetsu / Scorpion)Close the distance, blade work
shooterGunner / Shooter (Asteroid / Viper)Bullets, zoning, volume of fire
sniperSniper (Ibis / Lightning)One big shot from far away
trapperTrapper (Spider / Escudo)Traps, control, setup

Building a loadout of Triggers and picking a Black Trigger genuinely scratches the "assemble my trigger set" itch. The Black Triggers (Ashbringer, Aegis Core, Gravebind, Solar Flare…) feel appropriately like a whole extra character bolted onto one operator, which is exactly how a Black Trigger should feel. And the newest battle log actually explains what happened in plain words now, which as a casual player I really needed - before, it looked like a calculator threw up.

Where it singsThe theme, the naming, the squad-of-three fantasy, and the loadout-building. If I'm here for the World Trigger vibe, the surface delivers.

But the World Trigger soul is only half here

What makes a World Trigger fight thrilling isn't stat blocks - it's space, Trion management, and teamwork under a no-death rule. Three of those pillars are underbuilt:

1. There's no map - and WT is all about the map

In the manga, the entire drama of Attacker-vs-Sniper is closing the gap; Trappers wall off lanes; Shooters pin you in the open; snipers need line of sight and get hunted for it. Here, combat is two rows of three trading blows - a party-vs-party JRPG battle, not a spatial skirmish. My operators have "melee" and "ranged" tags but range never creates a decision - nobody has to get anywhere. That's the single biggest thing standing between "World Trigger flavored" and "feels like World Trigger."

2. Nobody Bails Out - they just die

The defining rule of Border combat is Bail Out: when your Trion body is destroyed you're ejected safely, not killed. It lowers the stakes of any single hit and makes the fight about objectives and attrition, not fear of death. Here a downed operator is flatly DEFEATED and gone. Permadeath-in-a-match is a fine design, but it's not the World Trigger feeling, and it makes the swingy one-shot attacks (a sniper deleting someone in a hit) feel extra harsh.

3. Trion is a number, not a personality

In the story, a person's Trion capacity is destiny - it's literally Osamu's central struggle. The game has a Trion Capacity stat and a Trion Affinity stat, but because income each turn is a semi-random tier (10/20/35) and most turns you cast one ability anyway, I never feel my Trion capacity defining me. The resource that should be the heart of a WT game is currently just a soft gate.

Also missing: Side EffectsWT's Side Effects (Jin's future-sight, Konami's danger sense) are iconic. The perk system is right there as the home for them - and one perk, Zheng's Foresight (reroll an attack), is basically Jin-lite. Lean into that framing and casual fans will grin.

The honest barrier: it asks a lot before it's fun

I'm the player who watched the anime and wants to hop in. This game hands me a d20 combat system, ~60 status effects, a Team Efficiency Grade with six sub-scores, a FAT mechanic with cooldown penalties, and a Trion tier roll - before I've thrown a punch. That's a lot of homework for someone here for the vibe. I don't need it removed; I need it taught, slowly, in a story.

  • Let the tutorial/story mode introduce one pillar at a time (Trion → Triggers → the squad → FAT → the Grade).
  • Keep leaning on the new plain-English log and the clickable ability/character popups - that's the difference between "opaque" and "learnable."
  • Give me a reason to care about each stat in words ("Higher Trion Affinity = more reliable income"), the way the Team Spirit readout now does.

My casual-fan bottom line

  1. The costume is fantastic. Names, classes, Black Triggers, squads - 8.5/10 fidelity.
  2. Add a spatial pillar (even a simple 3-lane / front-back / range-band system) so Attacker-vs-Sniper becomes a real WT dance.
  3. Consider a Bail-Out option (a downed-but-not-out state) to match the fiction and soften one-shots.
  4. Make Trion capacity feel defining, and frame perks as Side Effects.
  5. Tutorialize. The depth is a feature if the story teaches it one beat at a time.
