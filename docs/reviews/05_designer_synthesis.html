Isekai Strategem · Design Director Report · Synthesis of Reviews 1-4

Design director's synthesis & solution set

Reviewer background: systems & combat design on party-scale RPG combat (BG3 lineage), 4X tactical battles (Age of Wonders 4), and large-scale real-time tactics (Total War: Warhammer 3).

Executive diagnosis

I read the four player reviews and then went through the systems myself. Here is the headline, and it is genuinely encouraging: Isekai Strategem has a stronger systems-and-content foundation than most games at its stage, and a weaker combat-math and battlefield foundation than it needs. The counter engine, unique behaviors, combo recognizer, Team Efficiency Grade, and the queue/6-phase resolution are real, thoughtful, differentiated systems - the hard, creative part. What's underbuilt is the arithmetic the whole thing rests on, and the battlefield the whole thing happens in. Those are the cheap fixes, structurally - mostly re-tuning plus one or two systemic additions, not a rebuild.

Problem 1 - Restore bounded accuracy (P0, the keystone fix)

Diagnosis. Attack modifiers sit around +26 while Defense sits around +11; damage is dice-plus-a-huge-flat (2d6+37, 2d6+45, 4d8+86). A d20 swings ±19, but a 15-point pre-roll gap and a +45 flat mean the die contributes ~15% to to-hit and ~16% to damage. The game looks like a dice game and plays like deterministic arithmetic with a cosmetic roll - which is why advantage/disadvantage, crits, and the defender's roll all feel muted (D&D review), and why planned lines still "whiff" without earning tension (NA/Tactics reviews).

Why it's the keystone. Almost every other complaint is downstream. Fix the accuracy band and your existing advantage system, crit system, and to-hit tension all switch on for free. This is the BG3 lesson: 5e's bounded accuracy is why a d20 roll is dramatic at every level.

Concrete solution Compress the accuracy band ~3-4×. Target: the typical Attack−Defense modifier gap should fall inside the d20's range, so the roll is usually 40-60% of the outcome.
  • Attack modifier range ≈ +4 … +14; Defense modifier range ≈ +2 … +12 (from today's ~+26 / ~+11).
  • Rebalance damage so dice ≈ 40-50% of average. E.g., twin_fang_strike 2d6+37 (dice = 16% of 44) → ~5d6+21 (avg ≈ 38.5, dice ≈ 45%). Keep averages within ±10%; just move value from the flat bonus into more/bigger dice.
  • Decide: static Defense (AC-style) or opposed rolls. I recommend static Defense - it halves variance and log noise and is what bounded accuracy assumes. Keep opposed only if you want a defender-side build axis, and only after compression.
This is a spreadsheet pass over 60 triggers + stat blocks, not new code. It is the single highest-leverage change available.

Problem 2 - Add a spatial pillar (P1)

Diagnosis. Two of four reviewers (and the genre) want space. "Melee/ranged" are currently inert tags. Without a positional axis, an entire decision family - engage/hold, group/split, protect the back line - doesn't exist, and the World Trigger fantasy (Attacker closing on a Sniper) can't be expressed.

Concrete solution - you don't need a grid Add a front/back row + range-band layer, the lightest thing that creates real decisions (see: Darkest Dungeon rank system, Banner Saga's line, TWWH3's front-line/artillery logic distilled):
  • Rows: each squad has a Front and Back row (assign at draft, swap costs an action). Melee triggers hit the enemy Front row unless it's empty; Snipers/Shooters can reach the Back row. Front-row operators soak for the back.
  • Range bands (even simpler alternative): Close / Mid / Far. Attackers want Close (bonus), Snipers want Far (bonus, penalized Close), Shooters flexible; an action can change band. Now "close the gap" is a real tempo cost, and Trappers can lock a band.
This reuses your existing melee/ranged tags as inputs and turns them into choices. It is the biggest identity upgrade you can make.

Problem 3 - Make initiative earned, and soften lethality (P1)

Diagnosis. First-turn is decided by a 50/50 coin flip, and a single attack can delete a unit. The most decisive variable is pure luck - the antithesis of "strategem."

Concrete solution
  • Weight initiative by an earned quantity. You already compute the Team Efficiency Grade; use it (or a Trion-Affinity/Team-Spirit composite) to bias the flip - e.g., the higher-Grade squad wins the opening ~65/35, or spends a resource to seize it. Randomness the player can build against, per the tactics review.
  • Add a downed-but-not-dead state ("Bail Out"). A defeated operator drops to a Bailed state for 1-2 turns (out of the fight, can't be finished) instead of instantly gone. This directly answers the World Trigger fan, softens the one-shot problem, and converts matches from "delete race" to "attrition + objectives." It also makes going-second survivable, reducing the sting of the initiative flip.

Problem 4 - Put status effects and triggers on explicit budgets (P1)

Diagnosis. The condition library is rich but un-costed: marked is +50% damage-taken for 1 turn, exposed +25% for 2, petrified halves damage for 2; DoTs range 8→12/turn with no rule; crit chance scales toward ~75%. Players can't intuit relative value, and the AI can't value them either. On the trigger side, a handful of outliers dominate.

Solution A - a Status Point (SP) budget Cost every status as SP = magnitude-weight × duration × targets, and cap the SP a single trigger may carry relative to its Trion cost. Illustrative weights (per target per turn):
Effect classExampleSP / target-turn
Hard CC (no actions)stunned, frozen, charmed3.0
Damage-taken multipliermarked, exposed (per +25%)1.5
DoTbleeding/scorched (per 8 dmg)1.2
Stat swingweakened, fatigued (per ~10%)0.8
Economy denialsapped, silenced1.0-1.5
Then marked (+50% = 3.0 SP × 1 turn) and exposed (+25%×2 = 3.0 SP) land on the same budget - now they're intelligible and interchangeable, and a trigger applying a 3-target DoT is visibly "spending" 3× as much.
Solution B - a Trigger Value budget (and re-cost the outliers) Define Value = (avg_damage + SP_value) ÷ Trion_cost, then multiply by a cooldown factor (cd 1 = ×1.5, cd 2 = ×1.0, cd 3 = ×0.8) to price spam. Target a band (say 2.0-3.0 effective). Today's offenders, with fixes:

Problem 5 - Make Trion the beating heart it pretends to be (P2)

Diagnosis. Two reviewers noted Trion is thematically central but mechanically soft: income is a semi-random tier (10/20/35), most turns you cast once anyway, and capacity rarely bites.

Concrete solution
  • Deterministic (or tight) income scaled hard by Trion Affinity, so building for economy is a reliable, expressive choice (Naruto-Arena's deterministic chakra is the north star).
  • Make capacity gate burst: FAT's 3-ability turn should be bounded by capacity, so high-Trion operators are the ones who can unleash - the World Trigger "Trion is destiny" fantasy.
  • Elevate denial as a counter axis: sapped/genjutsu_trapped/silenced become a real sub-game if income and capacity actually matter.

Problem 6 - Tutorialize the depth (P2)

The World Trigger fan is right that the game front-loads a d20 system, ~60 statuses, a six-sub-score Grade, and FAT before the first punch. The recent battle-log rework (plain-English breakdowns, clickable ability/character popups) is exactly the right instinct - extend it: a story/tutorial that introduces one pillar per beat (Trion → Triggers → squad → FAT → Grade), and word-based stat explanations everywhere (as the Team Spirit readout already does). Depth is an asset once it's learnable.

Closing note to the team

Don't let the length of this list read as "the game is bad" - it isn't. You've built the expensive, creative layer (differentiated counters, uniques, combos, a genuinely clever risk/reward Grade, a real planning-phase resolution) that most projects never reach. The gaps the four audiences found are, almost entirely, tuning and two focused systemic additions. Fix bounded accuracy first (it's the keystone that lights up systems you already own), give the fight a space and an earned first move, and put your effects on a budget. Do those and you'll have converted a promising systems toy into a game all four of these very different players would keep playing.
